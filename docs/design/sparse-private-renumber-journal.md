# Standalone-Mongo private address migration (local prototype design)

Status: implementation contract, not deployed or advertised. Base f64ba2e. Sparse remains default-off. This supersedes the earlier design's prohibition on automatic address changes: enrolled identity and DNS are stable; literal old IP addresses are not stable identity.

## Actual gap

private_networks::allocate_locked expands pools but does not move existing conflicting assignments. save changes preferences while retaining old peer addresses. The process-local private_network_lock covers allocation/enrollment/save, not sync publication or every credential/member/ACL writer. Consequently a journal plus sequential peer writes alone would expose mixed generations and could republish obsolete authority. A mutex is not crash durability. No replica set, transaction, change stream, or atomic multi-document operation is assumed.

## Durable authority fence

One workspace head document owns monotonically increasing generation and optional migration ID. An atomic journaled head transition Stable(g) -> Applying(g+1,job,complete-plan-v2) is the linearization point. Reads/publications and all address/namespace writers must participate in a per-workspace in-process publication mutex and read the head fresh; an active job refuses new private authorization with explicit retry/migrating response (not auth denial and not old-snapshot fallback). Restart discovers complete active plans and recovers them; fresh durable-head checks keep affected workspaces fenced throughout recovery. Cross-process workers require database fencing, not this single-core mutex. Horizontal API support remains out of scope.

Already published authorities must be revoked: every incident Hub/base/pair ticket, signaling queue and existing carrier must bind workspace generation. Retiring the generation cancels all old transports, including authorized stream optimization and relay fallback. Wait for acknowledgements or maximum independent lease lifetime before editing addresses; acknowledgement alone cannot extend an old lease. Existing clients lacking migration/rebind support receive actionable update-required before migration starts. Legacy clients below4096 retain their existing contract absent migration. Never truncate peers or restore oversized legacy snapshots.

## Durable journal layout and ordering

Head contains schema, workspace, generation, state, job ID, old/new pool digest, assignment count/digest, preparation cursor and applied cursor. Separate immutable assignment rows keyed job+peer contain old/new canonical IPv4 plus stable machine/workspace identity and credential-generation pin. Do not store credentials. Chunk rows and bounded cursor scans avoid16MiB BSON and RAM exhaustion; upper bounds65536 peers and32 pools remain explicit. Create unique job+peer and job+new-IP indexes; ordinary private_peers workspace+IP unique index remains unchanged.

1. Select disjoint new pools avoiding all foreign reserved pools/assigned addresses and fresh own advertised LAN/VPN/WSL prefixes. Do not route over an ambiguous LAN. Hold allocation and workspace guards across staging. Do not activate a head or reserve targets before its entire immutable plan is durable; incomplete rows are inert and may be discarded safely after a crash. No peer address write can precede active-head publication.
2. Build immutable assignment rows using keyset pagination. Record generation/credential-generation, not full mutable peer blobs. Finalize count and canonical digest only after all rows durably exist. Atomically CAS the stable head to Applying with plan_version2, generation, job, targets and digest; this activates reservations and fences all publication. Other allocators include active reservations after restart. A pre-activation crash has no address writes or active reservation; a post-activation crash always has a complete journal. Never infer completeness from cursor alone.
3. After active-head publication and old-authority drain, revalidate target reservations and relevant local networks. Client migration/readiness negotiation remains a required integration before any public automatic trigger. Each peer CAS changes ONLY ip and address_generation with filters for stable workspace/machine/peer, expected old address and credential-generation. A row already at exact new address+job generation is idempotent success. Any other address/generation or missing/replaced identity blocks recovery; never overwrite foreign changes. Concurrent revocation remains allowed: disabled credentials stay disabled; migrating must never recreate credential fields or resurrect deleted peers.
4. Crash between peer CAS and row progress acknowledgement is safe: read exact assignment, detect AlreadyApplied, then acknowledge. Advance durable cursor only after assignment ACK. No write rollback to old IPs once Applying: recovery rolls forward because old pools may conflict and clients may have observed a new generation.
5. Verify every surviving expected peer's exact assignment and all reservation digests using bounded scans. Atomically publish new pool head Stable(g+1) only when complete; invalidate/rebuild DNS directory. New sync rebinds own IP and creates new independent pair epochs. DNS names/custom aliases and rules/groups/selectors are untouched. No old-IP aliases or synthetic peers.
6. Retain audit/tombstone and retire old reservations only after stable head commit plus old authority drain. Reservation GC cannot remove active/Preparing job reservations. Unknown job/head mismatch stays fenced with actionable repair, never guesses a prefix.

Every durable transition uses explicit acknowledged journaled writes (j:true); validate deployment journaling support and fail closed on unsupported writes. Do not assume majority replication on standalone. Crash recovery must reconcile uncertain write results by fresh exact reads. Process death is the fault model; disk loss is not recoverable by this journal alone.

## Required wiring before enabling

- private_networks allocator/ensure/save and routes/private_enrollment: head/reservation-aware allocation.
- routes/private_sync and private_sparse directory/pair publication: fresh head generation and publication fence before/after awaited reads; cached names never authorize.
- private_relay tickets/base/pair/carrier: workspace generation revocation and no late publication across head transition.
- credential/bootstrap/revoke, workspace/member/group/rule and machine name/domain mutations: audit each writer; preserve concurrent authoritative denial and reject address-affecting writers during migration.
- agent protocol: bounded conflict report containing validated prefixes, generation acknowledgement, own-IP clean rebind; all3OS owned aggregate routes/resolvers revalidated and old routes retired.
- startup recovery blocks affected workspace before normal serving; global allocator loads pending reservations before enrollment.

## Implementation boundary

A pure recovery decision core can be implemented/tested independently now. Real Mongo journal orchestration must not be presented as complete until durable head/reservation gates and publication/drain are wired together. Current f64 has none of these durable migration gates. No migration route or auto-trigger is enabled by adding the core. Exit/Tor/fairness and existing native disposable-host proof remain separate open requirements.

Primary references: [MongoDB single-document atomicity](https://www.mongodb.com/docs/manual/core/write-operations-atomicity/), [write concern](https://www.mongodb.com/docs/manual/reference/write-concern/), and [WiredTiger journaling](https://www.mongodb.com/docs/manual/core/journaling/). Multi-document update operations are not a transaction; this design deliberately uses a fenced roll-forward protocol.

## Local implemented checkpoints

The feature-gated publication fence now serializes sync, explicit and automatic enrollment, bootstrap, hostname changes, manual pool edits, and peer enable/disable/revoke. Queued sync and bootstrap re-read exact identity after acquiring the guard. Hub admissions and leases bind authenticated workspace explicitly (not peer-ID spelling); a generation transition retires old tickets, leases, pairs and connections. Real standalone-Mongo queued-sync tests cover address migration, revoke and credential rotation. Workspace generation metadata currently has a separate16384 retained-workspace bound; lifecycle reclamation remains to be resolved before scale claims.

The journal preparation implementation requires an unforgeable exclusive guard holding allocation then workspace locks. It verifies the complete peer inventory and immutable bounded plan, stages every immutable row first, and atomically activates an Applying plan_version2 head/reservation only after the entire plan is durable. This supersedes earlier frozen head-first Preparing prototypes, whose incomplete rows cannot provide automatic recovery without the original caller plan. Such old experimental versions remain explicitly fenced and are never silently adopted. Retrying after process-state loss requires exact job/digest/row identity. Pending head reservations participate in other tenants' allocations. A real standalone-Mongo test recreates App, proves ordinary admission stays blocked, resumes idempotently, rejects a foreign job/tampered row, preserves the entire peer document, and rejects another tenant's attempt to use the reserved pool.

Assignment application now performs a real monotonic16s drain, exact identity/address/credential CAS and journaled progress; a crash after peer ACK before progress resumes without recreating credentials or changing disabled status. Final publication uses a journaled Committing intent, exact old-preference CAS and job/generation markers, directory invalidation, then stable-head commit. An ACK lost after preferences is recoverable without overwriting a foreign edit. These internal stages remain unreachable from public routes; automatic conflict planning, startup scheduling and client readiness negotiation are still open. An interrupted test job is never left in production; all real tests used unique temporary databases/containers removed afterward.

**Authority-retirement blocker:** earlier16s waits prove the synthetic Mongo journal/crash phases only. They do not prove native authority retirement. Engine::install accepts up to60s remaining from client wall time, and std::time::Instant may exclude system suspend; delayed policy installation and resumed caches require a request-start-anchored, suspend-aware freshness/readiness protocol or explicit durable non-reuse until safe retirement. Raising a fixed sleep alone is not a solution. No automatic migration may be advertised or merged until this contract and native proof are complete.
